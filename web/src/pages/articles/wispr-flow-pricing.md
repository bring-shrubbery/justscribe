Wispr Flow has a free plan and three paid ones. This page sets out what each costs and includes as of October 2026, how the free plan's weekly limit works, what students pay, and how to cancel, all taken from Wispr's pricing page and help centre. Prices are in US dollars per user.

## The plans

| Plan | Monthly | Paid yearly | What it adds |
|---|---|---|---|
| Free | $0 | $0 | Dictation in any app and language, 2,000 words a week on desktop and 1,000 on mobile; dictionary and snippets; limited Notetaker history; ChatGPT and Claude integrations |
| Pro | $15 | $12 a month ($144 a year) | Unlimited dictation, longer Notetaker history, shared dictionary and snippets, a team workspace with central billing and usage analytics, priority support, early access to new features |
| Growth | $23 | $18 a month ($216 a year) | Single sign-on with SAML, HIPAA enforcement across the organisation, several email domains, admin control over model training, per-user usage reports |
| Enterprise | Custom, yearly only | | Free IT admin seats, model training locked off, SCIM provisioning, audit logs and MDM deployment, domain capture, custom contract terms, purchase-order billing, dedicated support |

The help centre also lists Growth with the Notetaker included at $33 a month or $312 a year.

Growth and Enterprise are for organisations. For one person the question is Free or Pro, and the difference between them is almost entirely the weekly limit.

## How the free limit works

- On a Mac or PC you can dictate **2,000 words a week**; on an iPhone or Android phone, **1,000**.
- Wispr counts the words you dictate, and only dictation. Using the Notetaker does not use up the allowance.
- The week **resets on Sunday at 12 a.m. Pacific time**. Unused words do not carry over.
- Flow warns you as you get near the cap and shows a card that blocks new dictations once you pass it. The block stays until Sunday; restarting the app or signing out and in again does not lift it. Now and then Wispr grants "bonus words" to keep you going for the week, at its discretion.
- A new install may start with a trial of Pro. When the trial ends you move to Free and the cap applies.

How far do 2,000 words go? It is about eight pages of typed text, or around a quarter of an hour of continuous speech at a conversational pace. That covers a day's emails and messages for many people, spread over the week. It does not cover drafting documents, writing long replies or talking to AI assistants by voice, which is what most people who pay are doing.

## What Pro is really for

Unlimited dictation is the reason to pay. The rest of the Pro list is useful mostly for teams: a shared dictionary so everyone spells the product names the same way, snippets shared across the company, one invoice and a usage dashboard. Command Mode, which rewrites selected text from a spoken instruction, is also limited to paid plans and trials while it is marked experimental.

Paying yearly brings Pro from $180 to $144 a year, a 20 percent saving, at the cost of committing for twelve months.

## Students, educators and nonprofits

Wispr's pricing page says students and educators with an education email address get 50 percent off Flow Pro, and that qualifying nonprofits get discounted rates. Its help centre separately describes a Student plan at $0 a year with everything in Pro. The two pages were written at different times, so check the [pricing page](https://wisprflow.ai/pricing) for the offer that applies when you sign up.

## Is Pro worth it?

Watch how often you hit the cap over a few weeks. If it is most weeks, you dictate enough that $12 to $15 a month is cheap against the time it saves, and the yearly plan is the better deal. If you rarely hit it, the free plan is the whole product, with every feature except unlimited volume. If the limit is the only thing in the way and you work on a Mac or PC, the dictation built into the operating system has no limit, and neither do on-device apps such as our own [JustScribe](/wispr-flow-alternative), which is free, Mac only, and needs no account.

## How to cancel

**Mac and Windows:** open Wispr Flow, go to Account → Manage plan, choose Cancel Subscription and confirm. The plan card should then show "Canceled" with "Plan ends soon" or "Plan ends in N days".

**iPhone:** Account → Manage plan → Plans → Cancel opens Apple's subscription sheet; cancel there. Apple, not Wispr, handles refunds for App Store subscriptions.

**Android:** Cancel plan opens Google Play; the cancellation happens in Google's subscription settings.

After cancelling you keep paid access until the end of the current billing period, then move to the free plan. Cancelling a trial stops it from converting into a paid subscription. There is no automatic prorated refund; ask Wispr support if you want one.

Two things to know before deleting your account rather than just cancelling:

- Deleting the account does not cancel an App Store or Google Play subscription. Cancel with Apple or Google first, or the billing continues.
- Deleting the account while a subscription billed by Wispr itself is active cancels it at once with no refund, not even for the unused part of the period. Cancel, and request any refund, before you delete.
